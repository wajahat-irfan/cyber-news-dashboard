Spot a phishing email

Phishing

Phishing messages pretend to be from a bank, a delivery service, or a colleague, and try to rush you into clicking a link or sharing a password.

  • Check the sender's full email address, not just the display name.
  • Hover over links before clicking — look for misspelt domains.
  • Never send passwords or one-time codes by email.
  • When in doubt, open the service's app or website yourself instead of following the link.

Strong passwords that you can remember

Passwords

A long password beats a short complicated one. Use a different password for every important account.

  • Use at least 12 characters — a short phrase works well.
  • Never reuse your email or banking password anywhere else.
  • Use a password manager instead of writing passwords on paper.
  • Change a password immediately if a service tells you it was leaked.

Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)

Two-Factor Auth

2FA adds a second check — usually a code from an app or a text message — so a stolen password alone is not enough to get in.

  • Enable 2FA on email, banking, and social accounts first.
  • Prefer an authenticator app over SMS codes when you can.
  • Save your backup codes somewhere safe, offline.
  • Never share a 2FA code with anyone, even if they claim to be support.

Keep software updated

Software Updates

Updates often fix security holes. An unpatched phone, browser, or app is one of the easiest targets.

  • Turn on automatic updates for your operating system and browser.
  • Update apps you actually use; remove ones you don't.
  • Restart your device when an update asks for it.
  • Only download software from the official store or the developer's own site.

Stay safe on public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi in cafés, airports, and hotels can be monitored by others on the same network.

  • Avoid logging in to banking or email on open Wi-Fi if you can wait.
  • Use your mobile data or a trusted VPN for sensitive tasks.
  • Make sure the sites you visit use HTTPS (the lock icon).
  • Turn off auto-connect to open networks and forget networks you no longer use.

Phishing by text message and call

Phishing

Scammers also use SMS ("your parcel is held") and phone calls ("your account will be closed") to create urgency.

  • Don't click links in unexpected texts — check the delivery in the official app.
  • Banks never ask for your full password or PIN on a call.
  • Hang up and call the organisation back on its published number.
  • Report scam messages to your mobile carrier if it offers a reporting option.
